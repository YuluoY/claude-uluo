# Paul Graham Writing Style Analysis

## Source Material
- "Do Things That Don't Scale" (2013)
- "How to Start Google" (2024)
- "Default Alive or Default Dead" (2015)
- "Maker's Schedule, Manager's Schedule" (2009)
- "Schlep Blindness" (2012)

## Style Markers

### Sentence Construction
- Average sentence length: ~15-18 words
- Sentences are short and declarative
- Rarely uses semicolons; prefers periods
- Uses simple subject-verb-object construction
- Avoids subordinate clauses and nested structures
- Employs rhetorical questions: "How could we start Google?"

### Paragraph Construction
- Short paragraphs, rarely more than 4-5 sentences
- Many paragraphs are single sentences for emphasis
- Paragraphs function as discrete logical units
- Each paragraph makes one point, then stops
- Uses white space liberally

### Signature Vocabulary
- "default alive" / "default dead"
- "do things that don't scale"
- "schlep blindness"
- "maker's schedule" / "manager's schedule"
- "the fatal pinch"
- "Collison installation"
- "scheming"
- "naive"
- "frighteningly ambitious"

### Essay Structure
1. Hook/anecdote opens the essay (personal observation, conversation, YC experience)
2. Core insight is stated clearly and early
3. Generalization: the insight applies beyond the example
4. Concrete examples: startups (Airbnb, Stripe, Facebook, Google, Apple)
5. Counterexamples or objections addressed
6. Practical takeaway: what the reader should do differently
7. Closing: often a punchy single-sentence paragraph

### Tone
- Conversational first-person: "I tell founders," "we advise," "when I talk to"
- Direct and unpretentious
- Slightly contrarian: challenges conventional wisdom
- No academic jargon, no business buzzwords
- Self-deprecating at times: "this sounds oversensitive"
- Uses "you" to directly address the reader
- Creates memorable named concepts

### Rhetorical Devices
- Analogies: startups as old cars with crank handles, newborns who seem useless
- Vivid comparisons: "like throwing an exception," "dotted outlines around missing things"
- Lists of exactly 3 reasons: "three things you need," "three reasons founders resist"
- Contrast pairs: maker's vs. manager's schedule, default alive vs. dead
- Anecdotes from Y Combinator experience

### Explanation Pattern
- Names a phenomenon first (coining a term)
- Defines it simply
- Gives concrete examples (usually startups YC funded)
- Explains why people miss it
- Provides the fix
- Closes with broader implication

### Key Thematic Domains
- Startups and Y Combinator
- Programming and technology
- Design and user experience
- Psychology of founders
- Business strategy and growth
- Education and learning
