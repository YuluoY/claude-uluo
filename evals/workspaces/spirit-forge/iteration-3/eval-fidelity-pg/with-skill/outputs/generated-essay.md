# Why AI Startups Must Be Default Alive

By a Paul Graham-style essayist, June 2026

I've been talking to a lot of AI founders lately. When I ask them the question I always ask -- are you default alive or default dead? -- most of them don't know the answer.

This is more alarming than it sounds.

AI startups face a specific version of the problem I wrote about in 2015. The compute bills are high. The models need constant retraining. And the revenue -- if there is any -- is not yet outpacing the burn. Most AI startups are default dead.

The founders don't realize it because they're counting on the next fundraise. They assume investors will keep writing checks because AI is the hot thing. But that assumption is exactly what makes it dangerous. The more you depend on investors to save you, the more fragile your position becomes.

There's a particular kind of schlep blindness at work here. Founders see the impressive demo, the excited users, the glowing TechCrunch coverage -- and they don't see the spreadsheet that says they run out of money in 14 months at current growth rates. Their unconscious mind won't let them see it.

The AI startups that survive will be the ones that do things that don't scale. Not fine-tuning another model. Not chasing the latest benchmark. But the boring, painful work of finding customers who will actually pay.

Here's the hard truth: there is surprisingly little connection between how impressive your AI is and how fast you grow. I see startups with breathtaking technology that can't find product-market fit, and startups with basically a wrapper around someone else's API that are growing 20% month over month. The difference is never the technology. It's whether the founders did the schleps.

So what should AI founders do?

First, know your numbers. Trevor Blackwell built a calculator for this. Use it. If you're default dead, the only conversation worth having is how to stop being default dead.

Second, resist the urge to hire. Hiring too fast is still the biggest killer of startups that raise money. A smaller team running lean can iterate faster than a bloated one burning cash on GPUs they don't need.

Third, find revenue now. Not next quarter. Not after the Series A. Now. Charge money for whatever version of your product exists today. You'll learn more from one paying customer than from a thousand free users.

The AI boom won't last forever. The startups that make it through will be the ones that treated being default alive as their first priority, not their last.

Don't be the founder who realizes too late that the fatal pinch already closed around you. Look at the numbers. Do the math. And if you're default dead -- fix it today.
